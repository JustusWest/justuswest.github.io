I am a Visiting Assistant Teaching Professor in the Department of Computer Science at Widener University. I received my Ph.D. in Computer and Information Science from Temple University, advised by Dr. Eduard Dragut.

My research focuses on thematic web data collection: building systems that assemble large, reusable collections of relevant documents scattered across many decentralized sources on the web. This work spans web crawling, information retrieval and LLM-based multi-agent systems. Most recently, I developed [PatchScout](/publication/NeurIPS2026) (NeurIPS 2026), a multi-agent framework that treats thematic collection as a long-horizon information foraging problem, and an [interactive, human-in-the-loop collection system](/publication/VLDB2026Demo) demonstrated at VLDB 2026.

This research grew out of an interdisciplinary NSF-funded project at Temple that uses AI to improve vocational and educational outcomes for autistic adults. As part of that project, I built [data science tools](/publication/JADD2025) to discover and characterize postsecondary autism support programs from their websites, and [interviewed students, parents, counselors and staff](/publication/Frontiers2026) about how autistic students find and use these programs.

At Widener, I teach Computer Structures and Assembly Language and Introduction to Programming in Python. You can find more on my [CV](/cv/) and [publications](/publications/) pages.
